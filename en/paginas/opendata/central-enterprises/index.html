1. Why Cross-Border Corporate Registries Require Schema Standardization

National corporate registers differ substantially across legal traditions and technical formats. Spain indexes legal entities through Registro Mercantil Central filings and CNAE-2009 activity trees; Switzerland publishes UID identifiers (`CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX`) and NOGA 2008 sectors via ZEFIX; the Netherlands coordinates KvK-nummer records under SBI 2008 taxonomy; France exposes national INSEE Sirene identifiers (`SIREN` and `SIRET`) classified by NAF/APE codes; Brazil maintains active corporate filings under Receita Federal `CNPJ`; Poland publishes KRS/REGON entities under PKD 2007; Indonesia tracks AHU/NIB filings under KBLI; and Malaysia records SSM corporate entities nationwide.

Without a shared protocol, data engineers, compliance analysts, and econometric researchers must write bespoke ingestion parsers, deduplication routines, and character-encoding normalizers for every jurisdiction. The Open Company Data Standard v1.1 technical specification solves this fragmentation by aligning all 21 markets to a single immutable tabular structure backed by SimHash entity deduplication, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country keys, normalized root domains, and transparent coverage metrics documented in the Central.Enterprises normalization methodology.

4. Open Source Distribution & Reproducibility

The machine-readable schema (`JSON Schema Draft 2020-12`), Frictionless `datapackage.json`, citation metadata (`CITATION.cff`), and 10 sanitized 100-row country samples are versioned publicly in the GitHub Open Company Data Standard v1.1.0 release repository and mirrored on the Open Company Data Standard GitHub Pages portal.
